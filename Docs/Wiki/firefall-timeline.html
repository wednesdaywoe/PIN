Timeline of the World

The road from a rock falling out of the sky to the war you fought as a recruit. Dates before the crash are approximate — the record never agreed with itself, and the game was in no hurry to settle it. Some entries below are things the game showed you and then refused to explain; those are marked apart on purpose.

Event Inciting incident Never explained Ground reclaimed

Before the eye of the storm

  • c. 2178 · The Firefall

    Fire falls from the sky

    An asteroid tracked as a near miss is caught by the Moon's gravity and comes down instead. The impact throws the world into a nine-year winter; the old powers, the United States among them, do not survive it. The game takes its name from this — the fire that rained down — though by the time you play, it is generations gone and half legend.

  • The dark age · after the impact

    Crystite is found in the ash

    In the debris of the impact, survivors find a blue crystalline mineral that gives up more energy than anything before it. Crystite becomes the thing the whole rebuilt world runs on — its power, its currency, and, eventually, its undoing. Everything downstream on this timeline traces back to the moment someone worked out what it could do.

  • A generation on · The Crystite Wars

    The scramble becomes a war

    A power source that good is also a prize worth killing for. Control of Crystite fractures the recovering world into open conflict — the Crystite Wars, referenced ever after as the fire everyone was rebuilt out of, and the one no one wants to light again.

  • The peace · The Accord

    The nations fold into one

    To keep humanity from burning its second chance the way it burned the first, the surviving states merge into the Accord — a single government whose founding job is to keep Crystite from starting another war. It is the flag you fight under, and it has been overstretched from the day it was raised.

  • The reach outward · Alpha Prime

    The Arclight is built

    The richest Crystite lies off-world, at Alpha Prime in the Alpha Centauri system. To hold that supply line the Accord builds the CMS Arclight — the largest ship ever made, able to fold space itself and cross the gulf in a single jump.

    The ships that ran the Alpha Prime route brought back more than cargo. Some of the wildlife you'll fight in the wastes never evolved on Earth.

The crash

  • Inciting incident
    c. 2233 · The maiden fold

    The Arclight comes down

    On its first fold, something fails. The jump tears — and something comes through the tear with the ship. Unable to complete the fold, the Arclight falls back toward the world it never finished leaving and comes down along the Brazilian coast.

    Its hull is still there. Half-submerged off Copacabana, too vast to move and too strange to pull apart, it sits on the horizon of everything that follows. Every job you take, every line you hold, happens in the shadow of that wreck. This is where the game begins.

    The officer who rode it down lived. The people he was bringing home did not. He commands the Accord's war now — a man steering the survivors of a disaster he was at the helm of.

What came out of it

  • Never explained
    After the crash · The Melding

    The Melding

    Out of the tear came the Melding: an energy storm the color of Crystite that spread across the sky and did not stop. It rewrites whatever it touches — stone, forest, animal, person — and it swallowed nearly the entire planet. Only the eye of it stayed clear.

    What it is, whether it thinks, whether it wants anything at all — no one who claimed to know was ever proven right. Walk too far into it and the world distorts, then dims, then kills you. That is the whole of what the game will confirm.

  • Never explained
    Shortly after · The Chosen

    The Chosen

    Then the storm gave up an enemy. The Chosen walked out of the Melding armed, organized, and fixed on a single purpose: the end of humankind. They field war-machines the size of buildings and they do not negotiate.

    Where they come from, what they were before, why they hate — the game never tells you, and the people fighting them never find out. They are simply the thing the storm sent.

Where you come in

  • Present day · New Eden

    The eye of the storm

    What's left of humanity holds a strip of survivable Brazil called New Eden, ringed by the Melding on every side. The Accord — gutted since the crash, far too few — cannot hold the line with soldiers alone.

    So it opens the ARES Initiative and hands power armor to anyone who will fight. A Battleframe: health that knits itself back together, jets to put you over the wreckage, wings to glide the rest. That's you — not the first to wear the number, not the last, just the one holding it now.

The war you fought

  • Razor's Edge · The Razorwind threat

    The Chosen get a name

    The faceless mass sharpens into tribes, and the one thrown at you first is the Razorwind. From your opening day ARES points you at them. Dredge, an outpost the Accord cannot afford to lose, comes under a Titan-led siege that takes a full platoon to hold. At Jericho you stand over a Melding Repulsor — the technology that physically holds the storm at bay — and defend it against wave after wave.

    Naming a tribe is not the same as understanding one. You learn how a Razorwind fights. You never learn what it is.

  • Ground reclaimed
    Razor's Edge → Devil's Due · The War Effort

    Into Devil's Tusk

    Down the coast lies Devil's Tusk, a lava-scarred zone the Melding took long ago — and buried beneath it, a pre-Melding weapons research facility the Accord means to reach first. The battlecruiser U.A.S. Vanguard anchors offshore to force the issue.

    Here you do the one thing that happens nowhere else in the game: you push the Melding back. Dark Crystite torn from the zone is refined into cores that feed a collective War Effort, and as the cores stack up the storm-line recedes and lost ground returns. A leaderboard keeps count of who bled most to move it.

    Every other front in the game is a holding action. This is the only map where the arrow points the Accord's way.

  • Devil's Due · The Chosen Prison

    The prison on the coast

    The Accord learns its missing people are being held in a Chosen Prison inside the zone, and the campaign builds to a battle in three parts: pull the captured scientists out under fire; then weather an all-out Chosen assault, working the coastal anti-air guns to knock Chosen ships out of the sky while a strike team flies transports into the prison itself — racing to free the prisoners before the Vanguard is lost beneath them.

    From here on

    This is as far as the shipped story reached before the servers went dark in 2017. Everything the reference wiki documents — the frames, the weapons, thumping, the fight for Devil's Tusk — lives inside these last few entries. The timeline ends where the game you played ran out.
