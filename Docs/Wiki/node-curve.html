Node cost curve

Five of ten nodes recovered. Costs climb by a roughly constant multiplier per node, so on a log axis the data lands close to a straight line and the gaps can be bridged by reading across. The multiplier isn't quite fixed: it's about 1.72× per node early and 2.0× per node at the top, which is why the line bends up slightly.

Estimates come from geometric interpolation anchored on the real numbers rather than one global fit. Nodes 3–5 bridge the known 8,000 → 70,000 gap at a constant 1.72× per step. Nodes 9–10 continue the 2.0× step measured across nodes 6 → 8. A single exponential over all five points gives XP ≈ 2,237 × 1.82n, which fits well but overshoots node 6 by 17% and undershoots node 8 by 9%, so it's the weaker basis for filling blanks.
