SetupBuild, deploy, launch

The terrain settings live in the deployed config and are deliberately not synced from the repo, whose copy still says false. Deploying cannot switch terrain off, but it is worth seeing the three lines with your own eyes once.

SetupThe zone came up whole

Zone 448 is 93 chunks and about 1.36 million pieces of solid geometry, read from a 13 GB cache in roughly six seconds. A chunk that fails is not an error — it is a warning and a piece of world that silently is not there.

What the run found that this sheet did not ask for

A Chosen shot the tester through a rock formation, with a hypothesis attached: that the world's ground and its separate objects behave differently. Chasing that offline — rebuilding zone 448's collision from the client's own map files — says the hypothesis is right in substance and wrong in mechanism. The ground ships as a mesh and converts without a single failure. Individual objects ship as a convex hull, a shape given by its corner points, and 252 of those convert to nothing: 235 produce an empty hull, 17 are rejected as malformed, and no other shape type fails anywhere in the zone. A failed object becomes a 1m placeholder at the chunk origin at height zero, about 400m under the basin floor, so nothing invisible blocks anything — the object is simply absent, and you can shoot through a rock that is visibly there. Filed as DATA-22.

One theory died on the way, and it is worth recording so nobody re-runs it. These map files store the world at five levels of detail and the loader reads only level 3, which looks exactly like where small scenery would be lost. Parsing a raw chunk file directly says level 3 is the only level that carries collision at all — 26.8MB of it in one chunk, against none in levels 0, 1, 2 and 4. The loader reads everything there is to read.

The rebuild is also the answer to "how do I regenerate the cache". All 93 chunks of zone 448 took about four minutes and wrote 5.6 GB:

export OPENSSL_ENABLE_SHA1_SIGNATURES=1 OPENSSL_CONF=$PWD/Tools/Session/openssl-legacy.cnf
dotnet build Tools/CollisionGenerator/CollisionGenerator.csproj -c Release
dotnet Tools/CollisionGenerator/bin/Release/net10.0/CollisionGenerator.dll \
  -c ~/Games/PIN/collision-cache \
  -m ~/.var/app/com.valvesoftware.Steam/.local/share/Steam/steamapps/common/Firefall/system/maps \
  -z 448

It runs in parallel, so its log lines interleave and per-chunk attribution from that log is not trustworthy — pass -j 1 if you need to know which chunk lost which object.

Why this order

Your own shots come before theirs. SOLID-WORLD-1 and SOLID-WORLD-2 need no creature, no hostility and no health, so they cost five minutes and they tell you whether the ray behaves before anything harder depends on it.

SOLID-WORLD-3 and SOLID-WORLD-4 are the same check twice, and they belong together. One proves shooting still works in the open, the other proves it stops behind cover. Either alone is ambiguous: a creature that never fires passes SOLID-WORLD-4 for the wrong reason.
